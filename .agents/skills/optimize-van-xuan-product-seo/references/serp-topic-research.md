# SERP and competitor topic research

Use current search results to understand language and intent, not as a source of
facts about Vạn Xuân products.

## Process

1. Search the exact product type plus its verified volume, alcohol level,
   ingredient, or use case. Try natural commercial and informational variants.
2. Review multiple genuinely relevant ranking pages. Ignore marketplaces,
   unrelated results, copied pages, obvious spam, and pages ranking only because
   the exact query has little competition.
3. Record recurring topic clusters: product type, volume, alcohol level,
   ingredients, production method, aroma/taste, suitable use context, serving,
   storage, provenance, packaging, price intent, wholesale intent, and shopper
   questions.
4. Compare those clusters with the verified Vạn Xuân fact sheet. Use only the
   intersection in product copy. Put missing high-value facts into the owner's
   fact-request list.
5. Find a useful original angle from first-party evidence: real production
   details, authentic images, variant clarity, transparent specifications, or
   practical buying guidance. Do not manufacture differentiation.

## Keyword handling

- Select one primary topic that matches the canonical product page.
- Use related terms only where they naturally answer a shopper question.
- Do not produce comma-separated keyword blocks, hidden keywords, doorway pages,
  or repetitive city/volume variants.
- Do not report search volume, difficulty, or ranking potential without naming a
  reliable measurement source and date.
- Competitor prominence is not proof that its claims, wording, or tactics comply
  with Google guidance.

## Evidence boundary

Competitor pages may support a topic recommendation such as “buyers compare
volume and alcohol level.” They may not support a Vạn Xuân claim such as a
specific alcohol level, ingredient, certification, taste, origin, aging time,
health effect, or production technique. Verify those from Admin data or an
owner-supplied source before publishing.

Save consulted URLs, query, access date, extracted topic clusters, and rejected
claims in the review artifact. Paraphrase findings; never copy competitor prose.
